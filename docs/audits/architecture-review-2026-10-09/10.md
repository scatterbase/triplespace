# Review: chapters 10 (pages and content models), 11 (rendering, templates and modules), 12 (files and media)

## Overall judgement

These three chapters are almost entirely beyond the first milestone, and the good news is that they are built so that the core (entity pages, resolved view, Action API) does not have to carry them: entity pages keep no `view.page` row and take their model from the registry; the `pages` partition is a separate source graph the entity path never writes; `wikitext.expansion` defaults to `off`, so the whole rendering machinery of chapter 11 (expander, refresh queue, manifests, Lua, Parsoid, `transclusion`/`render_state`/`entity_usage`) is dormant until a tenant turns it on; files are a separate partition payload with their own projection. What the core must honour on day one from this group is small and mostly registry data: `namespaces.toml` with Item/Property and the `entity-id` normalizer, `content-models.toml` with the entity models, `meta=siteinfo` reporting both, and the title resolver's entity branch (which is the API's ID resolution anyway). That separation is the right design and I would keep it.

Where it will hurt is at five seams where this group reaches back into the entity write path or into the page-ID space the API exposes, and at three places where the chapter form exposes mechanisms that cannot both be implemented as written. The seams: page IDs of mirrored entities come from a different sequence than tenant page IDs and so collide inside one tenant's `pageid` space (a core-use-case problem, not a rendering one); entity-usage invalidation is specified as a per-`resolved_version`-change classification that a Wikidata-scale sync would pay for every entity whether or not any page uses it; `commonsMedia` writes call Commons synchronously; file usage "by statement" implies an index over every `commonsMedia` value in the resolved view; and on-demand MediaInfo mirroring lets a page view write a mirror partition. The structural traps: three `view` tables with two writers chosen by a tenant setting; a blob-reference projection whose replay destroys bytes; a title index that is a join across two partitions with no stated replay order; moves that must create a `wikitext` redirect in namespaces whose registry entry forbids `wikitext`; and a "local overlay on an inherited file" that chapter 13 explicitly refuses for pages. None of these forecloses the incremental path if decided now; several become permanent if the record formats they touch (`scatter:v0/page`, `scatter:v0/changeset`, header field 9) ship first.

Sound and sequenced as I would sequence it: 10 §4 (content-model registry and the source/trait split), 10 §2.1–2.4 (one resolver, one hop, `redirect=no`), 11 §1.4 (`ExpandHost`, a pure expander), 11 §3.2 (render-scoped Parsoid endpoint), 12 §1.3 (upload record commits to bytes without holding them), 12 §5.1 (the removal vocabulary), 12 §4.3 (signed URLs for non-public bytes). 11 §4.3's honesty that the render tables are not pure functions of the log is the right call and should stay.

### F-10-1. Mirrored entities and tenant pages share one `pageid` space but draw from two sequences

**Chapter §:** 10 §3.1 (page IDs "minted by the instance in sequence, start at 1"), with 03 §2.3 and §4.5, 04 §5.3, 05 §6.4, 08 §2.1.
**Severity:** blocker for the core use case.

**What the text says.** 10 §3.1: "Page IDs are minted by the instance in sequence, start at 1 and are never reused." 03 §2.3: page IDs are "one set per tenant", but "A mirrored entity's page ID is taken from `log."instance.page_id"` the first time its key is written, since it belongs to no tenant." 03 §4.5: "Entity pages get their `page_id` from the same sequence as document pages, so `view.entity.page_id` and `view.page.page_id` never collide and one `pageid` space covers both kinds of page." 04 §5.3: a local page ID "comes from the tenant's sequence below 2^40", and only page-repository pages are provider-ranged.

**Why it is a problem.** On any tenant that reads Wikidata, `Item:WDQ42` reports a `pageid` from the instance sequence and `Main Page` reports one from the tenant sequence; both start at 1 and both stay below 2^40, so the same number names two pages in one tenant's API. `pageids=`, `generator=` output, `prop=info`, `list=backlinks` (`page_link.target_id`), thread attachment (`{kind: page, id}`), `view.page_prop`'s FK and the `M{page ID}` derivation all assume a tenant-unique `pageid`. The chapters say three incompatible things ("the instance", "per tenant", "same sequence") and the one that is implemented decides the record header (field 9), which is inside the Merkle tree and therefore permanent. This bites on the very first milestone: a Wikidata mirror plus one local page.

**Suggestion.** Decide once, before any mirror record is written: give mirrored entities a provider-ranged page ID exactly as their revision IDs are ranged (`provider_number << 40 | upstream page ID` where the provider exposes one, as Wikidata does; `provider_number << 40 | per-provider counter` otherwise), and reserve the sub-2^40 range for the tenant's own sequence. That makes 04 §5.3's "never collides" true for entities as well as repository pages, removes `instance.page_id` from the hot ingest path, and keeps mirror records allocation-free in bootstrap mode. Correct 10 §3.1 to say "per tenant" and 03 §4.5 to say what the ranges are.

### F-10-2. Entity-usage invalidation is specified as work on every entity change, not on every used entity

**Chapter §:** 11 §4.4 trigger 3 and §7.9; 10 §5.2.
**Severity:** design debt that will be expensive later (a day-one cost if it lands in the resolution projection).

**What the text says.** "When an entity's `resolved_version` changes, the change is classified from its structured diff: which languages' labels and descriptions, which properties' statements, which sitelinks, and whether it was a redirect or a change of cluster. Only pages whose aspects match get an epoch bump and a refresh."

**Why it is a problem.** The sentence orders the work backwards. A Wikidata mirror changes hundreds of entities a second during sync and 100M during bootstrap; on a Wikibase-focused tenant almost none of them is used by any `wikitext` page. If the classification runs where the text implies (wherever `resolved_version` is bumped, i.e. the `entity` projection in step 4), the entity write path pays a structured diff per change forever, for a table that is usually empty. The text also does not say whether this runs inside the appending transaction, in the fan-out budget, or as a consumer of the event stream of 03 §7; nor what happens in bootstrap mode; nor how `view.entity_usage`, "with canonical IDs", is re-keyed when a cluster merge changes the canonical ID (10 §5.2's `page_link` rows "after resolution" have the same problem; 03 §4.7 refreshes `page_link` targets on title changes but says nothing about cluster changes).

**Suggestion.** State the order: (1) an indexed existence check on `view.entity_usage (entity_id)` for the canonical ID and every member of its cluster; (2) only on a hit, compute the aspect diff; (3) do this as an asynchronous consumer of the event stream, never inside step 4, and not at all while a partition is in bootstrap mode or while no tenant on the instance has `wikitext.expansion = on`. Key `entity_usage` and `page_link` entity rows by the ID as written in the page plus the canonical at render time, and have the cluster projection enqueue a refresh for pages whose rows name a merged member rather than rewriting rows.

### F-10-3. Three `view` tables have two writers, chosen by a tenant setting, and no owner for the switch

**Chapter §:** 10 §5.2, §5.3, §5.5; 11 §4.1, §4.3; 03 §4.7 and §6.1 step 2.
**Severity:** design debt that will be expensive later.

**What the text says.** 10 §5.3: with expansion off, category membership "is read from the text" by the page projection in step 2 (03 §4.7: "`page_category` and `category` run in step 2, with `page`"). 11 §4.1: with expansion on, "These rows are written by the refresh job, not by the page projection." The same holds for `page_link` (10 §5.2: "its rows are written by the refresh job") and for `view.page_prop`, where 10 §5.5 splits properties between "the page projection, in step 2, synchronously" (`templatedata`, `defaultsort`, `hiddencat`) and "the refresh job" (`displaytitle`, `wikibase-shortdesc`, `disambiguation`, the switches), and then says "A property the latest render did not set is deleted." 11 §4.3 adds that with expansion on these tables are "rebuilt by re-rendering", i.e. no longer replayed.

**Why it is a problem.** One table, two writers, and the writer is a runtime setting: (a) the refresh job's "delete what the render did not set" will delete the projection-written `templatedata`/`defaultsort` rows unless the job re-derives them, which the text implies but does not state; (b) with expansion *off* there is no refresh job, so `displaytitle`, `wikibase-shortdesc` and `__DISAMBIG__` are "implemented" in the registry (11 §2.2) but nothing writes their page property; (c) flipping `wikitext.expansion` changes which writer owns rows already in the table and which rebuild procedure (replay vs re-render) is correct, and 23 §3's "bumps the render epoch of every wikitext page and queues them for refresh" is an O(pages) job the chapter never names as one; (d) a rebuild from offset 0 on a tenant with expansion on will populate `page_category` from the projection in step 2 and then have the refresh job overwrite it, or not, depending on which runs first.

**Suggestion.** Pick one writer per table regardless of the setting: let the page projection always write the source-derived rows (what the stored text says) and let the refresh job always write the expansion-derived rows, either in separate tables (`page_category_src` / `page_category_expanded`, unioned by readers) or in one table with a `from_render boolean` column that each writer owns exclusively. Then expansion off simply means the render-owned rows are empty, the setting flip is "truncate render-owned rows and enqueue", and rebuild semantics are the same for every tenant. State explicitly that the refresh job never deletes a row it did not write.

### F-10-4. The blob reference count is a projection whose replay destroys bytes

**Chapter §:** 12 §5.3; 12 §3.4 ("Like every `view` table it is rebuilt from the log"); 03 §6.1 ("A rebuild truncates the projection's tables and replays from offset 0").
**Severity:** blocker for the files feature (not for the core), and a permanent-loss trap.

**What the text says.** "`view.blob` counts, per scope and hash, the upload records whose content part is not erased … When an erasure brings the count to zero, the projection queues the object and its thumbnails for deletion at once, not at the next sweep."

**Why it is a problem.** A projection with an external side effect is not replayable. Replaying from offset 0 walks through every historical state: an upload (count 1), its erasure (count 0, delete queued), then a later re-upload of the same bytes under a different page (count 1 again). During the replay the delete fires while the live head still references the bytes. The same applies to a partial rebuild after a crash, and to the `files/{repo}` mirror partition where compaction "erases tombstoned records" so that a replay sees a different sequence than the original run did. 12 §1.3's "revert stores nothing" compounds it: version 1 erased for privacy keeps its bytes alive through version 3's identical hash, which the erasure form does not say.

**Suggestion.** Make `view.blob` a pure count and make destruction a separate `ops` job that runs only against the live head: it takes a hash whose count reached zero, re-checks the count at the current head under a lock, and deletes. Mark the projection as "no side effects during replay" in 03 §6.1 and say that a rebuild re-enqueues nothing. Have the erasure form (and `ts-erase` on a version) report when other unerased versions in the same scope share the hash, so a privacy erasure is not silently ineffective.

### F-10-5. The title index is a join across two partitions with no stated replay order

**Chapter §:** 10 §3.1 ("A title index is a projection"), 10 §2.5 ("Moving over a redirect"), 09 §6.2, 03 §4.5 (`page_title … WHERE NOT deleted`), 03 §6.1 (rebuild order), 16 §2.1.
**Severity:** design debt that will be expensive later.

**What the text says.** Deletion "is an ACL, not a page operation" written to the tenant `log` partition; the title index is a partial unique index on `view.page (ns, title) WHERE NOT deleted`, where `deleted` is derived from that ACL; a new page may take a deleted page's title (09 §6.2). Moving over a one-revision redirect writes the delete ACL (tenant `log`) and the `move` record (`pages`) "in the same transaction with one base check … so the title index frees the title before the `move` record takes it." 16 §2.1 says writes "cannot be made atomic across partitions."

**Why it is a problem.** Whether `create` at title X is legal depends on the interleaving of two partitions' records (the `pages` create and the `log` ACL). The live path sees them in commit order, but 03 §6.1 orders a rebuild "config, instance log, mirrors, then tenant partitions" and says nothing about the order *within* a tenant's partitions. Replaying `pages` before `log` hits the unique index on the second page ever created at a reused title; replaying `log` first applies ACLs to page IDs that do not yet exist. The same question decides whether `view.redirect.target_page_id` and `page_link` targets are correct mid-rebuild. 10 §2.5's atomicity claim is true in Postgres (01 §1.5 borrows the caller's transaction) and false under 16 §2.1's stated principle; one of them is the rule.

**Suggestion.** State that a tenant's partitions are replayed in the appending transaction's order (03 §7 already carries `txn` on every delta row; the log itself needs a per-tenant commit sequence or the `txn` recorded in `ops`) or, cheaper, that the `page` projection drops the partial unique index during rebuild and recreates it at the end, with `deleted` applied in a pre-pass over the ACL partition. Settle 16 §2.1 versus 01 §1.5: cross-partition atomic append is a guaranteed property of `LogStore` (and the file-segment backend must then say how), or 10 §2.5 must be re-stated as two records with a reserved page ID.

### F-10-6. A move must create a `wikitext` redirect in namespaces whose registry forbids `wikitext`

**Chapter §:** 10 §2.5 versus 10 §1.4, §2.3 and §4.4.
**Severity:** minor (contradiction), but it is a validation rule a programmer will invent alone.

**What the text says.** §2.5: a move appends "a `create` record for a new page at the old title, model `wikitext`, text `#REDIRECT [[New title]]`", with exceptions only for user renames, talk pages and thread renames. §1.4: Board (310) allows only `triplespace-board`; Table, Scope and Query allow `wikitext` "for titles ending `/doc`" only; Domain, Keyword, Notation, Item, Property allow only their entity model. §2.3: "`triplespace-table` and `triplespace-board` pages never are [redirects]."

**Why it is a problem.** `action=move` on `Table:Foo` → `Table:Bar` must create a `wikitext` page at `Table:Foo`, which the namespace catalogue says is not an allowed model at that title ("The registry rejects a `pages` namespace whose allowed models mix sources" is satisfied, but the per-title rule is not). Either the move is refused, leaves no redirect, or the registry is violated by the system's own write. The `ts-sprint-title` rule for Project subpages has the same shape (a sprint moved to a non-subpage title).

**Suggestion.** Add one rule to §2.5: a move leaves a redirect only where the old title may hold `wikitext` under §1.4; otherwise it behaves as `noredirect` without needing `suppressredirect`, and the `move/move` event says so. Say which namespaces disallow `move` altogether (entity, keyed-type, Thread, Board).

### F-10-7. "Local overlay on an inherited file" is the entry kind chapter 13 refuses, and its page ID has no source

**Chapter §:** 12 §6.5–6.6 versus 13 §1.7; 04 §5.3; 05 §6.4.
**Severity:** design debt that will be expensive later (it touches the `M{page ID}` contract and the change-set subject).

**What the text says.** 12 §6.6: "The local page's own `M{local page ID}` names the local overlay … with the ranged page ID of 0052 §6 where the file is only inherited." 13 §1.7: "A foreign page carries no local statements … a page with local statements and no local text would be a third kind of entry, neither inherited nor forked … Local metadata about a foreign page waits for a decision on that shape." 04 §5.3 and 03 §2.3 give provider-ranged page IDs only to pages served by a *page* repository, which has a provider number; a `file-repo` (12 §6.1) is a different config kind with no number.

**Why it is a problem.** For files, 12 §6.5's local page with text and statements and no upload is fine: it is a real local page with a real `create`. But 12 §6.6 then keys an overlay by a ranged ID for a file with no local page, which is exactly the third kind of entry 13 defers, and there is no rule that gives a `mediawiki` file repository a provider number from which to range. A fork (12 §9) or a later local page then changes the page ID under the overlay's statements, which chapter 13 names as the reason it refused the shape. The same section says the local `M` and the mirrored `WDM` are "one subject by construction, not a cluster": resolution (04, 05 §1.4) fuses by cluster, so "by construction" needs a second identity mechanism in the resolution projection (a `view.file.mediainfo_id` join) that no other subject type has.

**Suggestion.** Drop the ranged-ID overlay: local annotations on a foreign file require a local File page (a `create` with no upload, as §6.5 already allows), and the overlay is keyed by that page's `M` ID. Make the `M`–`WDM` identity either an ordinary cluster with a fixed canonical (so the existing resolver handles it) or an explicit, documented special case in the resolution projection fed from `view.file.mediainfo_id`. Align 13 §1.7 to say the same for pages when the decision is taken.

### F-10-8. Captions add a `terms` operation that the change-set format does not have, and key the subject two ways

**Chapter §:** 12 §8.4; 06 §5.1; 05 §3.2; 03 §4.3 and the catalogue row "page subjects (the page ID in decimal)".
**Severity:** minor, but it is a wire/log format decision and therefore permanent.

**What the text says.** 12 §8.4: captions "are written by a `terms` operation on the page's change set in `pages` — the change-set payload … gains terms for File pages only." 05 §3.2 lists the operations of `scatter:v0/changeset`: `put`, `tombstone`, `redirect`, `create`, `create-or-add`, `add`, `remove`, `override`, `retain`, `convert`, `same-as`, `different-from`, `equivalent-property`, `adopt`; `add` already "merges statements, terms or references onto any subject" and `override` "overrides the rank or a term". 03's catalogue keys page statements by "the page ID in decimal" in `statement_assertion`/`entity_ref`, while `view.term` keys captions by `M{page ID}`.

**Why it is a problem.** A new operation in the one change-set format is a format change that every path (ingest, bulk endpoint, edit API, federation adapter) must parse forever; `add`/`remove`/`override` of terms on subject `M{page ID}` already express captions with no new op. Two keys for one subject (decimal page ID in the statement tables, `M{id}` in `term`) is the kind of trap that surfaces as a join bug the first time a report crosses them.

**Suggestion.** Delete the `terms` operation; say captions are `add`/`remove`/`override` of `labels`/`descriptions` on subject `M{page ID}`, with the File-page-only restriction enforced at validation. Pick one subject key for page subjects across `view` (I would use `M{id}` for File pages and the decimal page ID elsewhere only if 06 §5.2 insists; better, one form everywhere).

### F-10-9. `commonsMedia` writes call Commons synchronously, from the entity write path

**Chapter §:** 12 §7.1.
**Severity:** design debt that hits the core use case as soon as a tenant edits items with images.

**What the text says.** "a local write is validated against it, as Wikibase validates against Commons; if the repository cannot be reached the write is refused with `upstream-unavailable`."

**Why it is a problem.** This is an upstream HTTP call inside a local edit (`wbsetclaim` with P18 via WikidataIntegrator, or a bulk job creating thousands of items with images), subject to the upstream client's rate limit, and a hard refusal when Commons is slow. Wikibase does the same, but Wikibase does not have a bulk-job path whose whole point is "append at whatever rate the storage can sustain" (00 §1.2). Nothing says the check is cached, batched, or skippable for jobs.

**Suggestion.** Cache existence per (repo, normalized name) in L1 with a TTL; validate in batches for change sets; let a job declare `validate_media = defer` so values are accepted and a tracking row (constraint-style, 03 §4.4) marks ones that later fail; keep the synchronous refusal only for the interactive edit API.

### F-10-10. File usage "by statement" and `mirror` mode imply an index over every media value in the resolved view

**Chapter §:** 12 §7.3 and §6.4.
**Severity:** design debt that will be expensive later; a Wikidata-scale cost if left implicit.

**What the text says.** §7.3: "A file's usage also includes the entities whose statements name it." §6.4: a mirror "mirrors the files that are used: referenced by a page of any tenant using the repository, or by a `commonsMedia` value in such a tenant's resolved view."

**Why it is a problem.** `view.file_link` is rows "from the latest revision of every page"; no table is named for usage by statement. Answering "which entities name this file" needs a reverse index over `commonsMedia`/`localMedia` values; for a `full` Wikidata mirror that is every P18/P154/P41 value, tens of millions of rows kept current by the resolution projection, and `mirror` mode then defines "used" as "every image Wikidata names", a terabyte-scale copy job the chapter does not name. 03 §4.4 already has `view.value_key` "populated only for those properties" a constraint names; this is the same table with a different trigger.

**Suggestion.** Say usage-by-statement is served from `view.value_key` populated for media data types only when a file repository is configured, and scope `mirror` mode's "used" to page usage plus statements in the *local* graph, with resolved-view usage an explicit opt-in that the setting's documentation prices.

### F-10-11. On-demand MediaInfo mirroring lets a page view write a mirror partition

**Chapter §:** 12 §6.6 versus 02 §1.2 / 05 §1.2 (mirror graphs "written only by that provider's sync jobs").
**Severity:** design debt that will be expensive later.

**What the text says.** "`mediainfo.mirror` (site, default `on-demand`): a Commons file's MediaInfo is fetched through `Special:EntityData` the first time the file is used by a local page or shown, written as a `put`, and kept current by Commons' EventStreams."

**Why it is a problem.** "Or shown" makes an anonymous GET perform an upstream fetch and an append to `mirror/WD`, under the render-scoped anonymous reader of 11 §3.2 and the single-flight render of §4.5. That is a read that writes (cache stampede on first view of a gallery, a job with no job record, an actor for the `put` that is not a sync job), and it contradicts the one rule the multi-source core depends on for provider isolation. It also means every tenant's page views feed one instance-wide mirror partition.

**Suggestion.** Make on-demand mean "enqueue a fetch for the shallow-mirroring job of 05 §5 (`ops.entity_fetch` already exists) and render without the statements until it lands", with the manifest recording a short-expiry miss exactly as 11 §7.10 does for unheld entities.

### F-10-12. No stale serving after a dependency-induced epoch bump

**Chapter §:** 11 §4.4 trigger 2, §4.5.
**Severity:** design debt; the first Wikipedia-fork tenant with Lua will meet it.

**What the text says.** "A miss renders synchronously under single flight; there is no stale serving after an epoch bump, as MediaWiki renders afresh after `page_touched` moves." Trigger 2 bumps the epoch of every page whose `transclusion` row names the changed page, "in batches".

**Why it is a problem.** An edit to a citation template on a fork of a large wiki bumps the epoch of most pages at once; every subsequent view of each is a synchronous render with Lua (7 s CPU budget per render, 11 §7.4) before any byte is served. MediaWiki survives this because PoolCounter serves the stale parser-cache entry when the pool is full and because `HTMLCacheUpdate` is throttled; the chapter adopts the first half of MediaWiki's behaviour and not the second.

**Suggestion.** Distinguish the page's own edit (render afresh, as now) from a dependency bump (serve the old `p:` entry with `stale-while-revalidate` and let the refresh queue re-render), and bound synchronous renders per tenant with a pool that falls back to stale under pressure. Keep the "no stale after erasure/hiding" rule of §4.5, which is a visibility rule and is different.

### F-10-13. Minor items worth a line each

- **10 §3.2 "Each revision stores the full text … Pages are small", with 10 §6 "Import every revision" and 13's history-seeded forks.** True for a Wikibase; not for a Wikipedia import, where full-history text is tens of terabytes and MediaWiki relies on external storage and compression. Say that imports of that size depend on packed storage (03 §3) and are not a milestone-1 claim.
- **11 §1.2: the parser's `Configuration` "is generated from `namespaces.toml`".** Tenant namespaces (3000+, entity sources) are records, not registry data, so the configuration must be built per tenant at runtime or `[[Foo:Bar]]` in a tenant namespace parses as a main-namespace link.
- **10 §5.5 `wikibase_item` "written by the sitelink projection"** with a FK to `view.page`: nothing says who writes it when the page is created after the sitelink. One line: the page projection re-checks `view.sitelink` by title on `create` and `move`.
- **12 §1.3 "A later upload appends only the `upload` record … no null text revision"** while 10 §3.2's `follow` *is* a null revision: fine, but `prop=revisions` must then synthesize MediaWiki's `rvslots=main` content for an upload revision; say so in 18.
- **12 §5.4 takedown "in one request, synchronously … in every scope"** needs an instance-wide `(sha256|sha1) → version` index across all tenants' `file_version` child tables; 03 §4.9 indexes per table. Name the cross-tenant index, since 08 §9's per-tenant storage domains otherwise keep tenants apart.
