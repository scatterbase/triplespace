# Architecture review, 2026-10-09

A top-to-bottom read of `docs/architecture/` (24 chapters, the current text of the 79 ADRs) with one question: if we build this, where will it hurt? The lens is the owner's: the spec is detailed so that engineering constraints are fixed up front rather than arriving as technical debt, while development is incremental and the next phase validates the core use case, a large-scale Wikibase with multiple sources (a Wikidata `full` mirror, local data, OpenAlex, on Postgres, with the resolved view, identity clusters, the Action and REST APIs that Pywikibot and WikidataIntegrator use, and the site UI).

Where things stand: milestone 1 (API-only single tenant, Librarybase adopted at 247k entities, internetdomains as a key-mapped provider, the three client libraries passing in CI) is built. `adoption-results-and-wikidata-projection.md` already measured the per-entity path at 345 entities/s and concluded that a full Wikidata load needs batch-shaped writes, deferred indexes, set-based bootstrap projections and a decision on mirror terms. This review takes those as given and looks at the rest of the design from the same vantage point.

Method: I read the core chapters (00, 01, 03, 04, 05) and had eight independent reviewers read every chapter in groups with the same brief (`docs/audits/architecture-review-2026-10-09/BRIEF.md`), then verified every finding quoted here against the chapter text. The eight detailed reports are in `docs/audits/architecture-review-2026-10-09/`, one per group (`01.md` for chapters 01 and 03, `04.md` for 04 and 05, and so on), with 110 findings in all. This document is the synthesis: what matters, why, and what I recommend deciding. Findings are cited as `F-01-3` (group 01, finding 3).

## 1. Overall judgement

The design is in good shape where it is hardest to change later. The log layer (canonical CBOR, RFC 6962 trees, salted erasable parts, compaction that keeps leaves, IDs inside the hashed header) is conventional and verifiable and should be built as written; the storage layer's big choices (log and projections in one database and one transaction, the blob serves the page and tables serve queries, keys that name versions, erasure as the only purge) are right; the identity model (who minted versus who asserts, one-to-one clusters with held conflicts, keyed types with surrogates, `adopt` with floors) is unusually well thought through; the API chapter is mostly servable from `view` without per-request work that grows with the mirror; and the later chapters (pages, rendering, files, discussions, structured pages, federation) are built so that the core does not have to carry them. Every reviewer, independently, said some version of "sound, build as written" about the majority of their chapters.

Where it will hurt is not in any one table or route but in five threads that run through many chapters at once, each of which the chapter form exposes because the same mechanism is now described in one place from several ADRs' angles. (1) The **shared view plus tenant overlay** model, which the whole multi-source design rests on, is undermined by three things specified elsewhere: canonical IDs are rewritten into the *stored* resolved view, so one cluster link fans out over every referrer per tenant; resolution policy (provider order, link properties, roles, normalizers, term languages) is tenant configuration, so two tenants with different policy cannot share a row; and the tables that hold clusters, entity sources and terms have no tenant column where the overlay rule needs one. (2) The **write path** has accreted inline work from many ADRs — resolution, terms, identifiers, references, constraints, the RDF delta, the activity row, ACL lookups, edit filters, entity-usage classification, scope matching, schema validation — all hanging off one appending transaction and one 250 ms budget, with the partition lock held throughout; that is the path a weekly Wikidata re-sync of ten million entities would take, and the only alternative specified is bootstrap into an empty partition. (3) **Rebuild** is specified as "truncate and replay from offset 0" for every projection, which is unaffordable for a mirror, unsafe for the two projections that have side effects, and silent about replay order across partitions and about concurrency between the inline path and the worker. (4) A handful of **permanent format decisions** are currently contradictory or unmade: which sequence mints a mirrored entity's page ID, how an actor key is spelled and where it lives, the bit budget of ranged revision IDs, the stored form of unit, globe and calendar IRIs, who attests the first records of the instance `config` partition. (5) The **instance/tenant scope** of configuration is wrong in about a quarter of the catalogue, by the 23 §3.1 "dotted keys are tenant site settings" rule and by inheritance from single-tenant ADRs: adapters write tenant property IDs into instance partitions, tenants hold capacity knobs, registry copies have no upgrade path.

None of this argues against the level of detail. The opposite: every one of these would have been found by a programmer, alone, at the moment of writing the resolution projection or the first mirror record, and resolved by whatever was expedient that day. The detail made them findable now. What the next phase needs is a short list of decisions (section 7), most of which are one paragraph each, and a few structural rules in chapter 03 (section 4) that several later chapters currently violate without noticing.

## 2. Decisions that are permanent once a mirror record is written

The log is append-only and nine header fields are under the Merkle tree, so these cannot be changed after the first production mirror record. The milestone-1 instance can be re-logged; the first Wikidata load should not have to be.

**R1. Page IDs of mirrored entities (F-01-3, F-10-1, F-13-1, found independently by four groups).** 03 §2.3 takes a mirrored entity's page ID from `log."instance.page_id"`; 03 §4.5 says entity and document pages share "the same sequence" so one `pageid` space covers both; 04 §5.3 says a local page ID "comes from the tenant's sequence below 2^40" and only repository pages are provider-ranged; 10 §3.1 says page IDs are "minted by the instance". Three of these cannot be true together. As written, `Item:WDQ42` and `Main Page` on one tenant can share a `pageid`, which breaks `pageids=`, `prop=info`, thread targets `{kind: page, id}`, `view.talk_page`'s unique key, scope members and `page_link`, and field 9 is hashed. Recommendation: mirrored entities take a provider-ranged page ID exactly as their revision IDs are ranged (`provider_number << 40 | upstream page ID` where the provider publishes one, as Wikidata does; `| mirror offset` otherwise), computed by the writer with no allocation; the sub-2^40 range is the tenant's own sequence. This also removes the projection read that every bootstrap writer currently needs to mint a hashed field, and it makes 04 §5.3 true for entities. Drop `page_id UNIQUE` on `view.entity` (shared and overlay rows share it) in favour of `(tenant, page_id)`.

**R2. Actor key spelling and placement (F-07-2).** 07 §1.1 says an actor key is `local:42` and "is what log headers carry"; 08 §3.1 says the same edit is attested by `librarybase:42` and `local` "is no longer an issuer code of its own"; 01 §2.3 puts the actor in the erasable attestation part, which is the only placement under which vanishing and attestation cuts can work. Recommendation: records carry `{tenant slug}:{id}` in the attestation part, never in the header (except as the key of actor records themselves); `local:` is a request-time alias; add to 08 §1.2 that a tenant slug, like the farm slug, is permanent once the tenant's first record exists; fix the 03 §4.5 index that filters on `issuer = 'local'`.

**R3. Ranged IDs above 2^53 (F-01-11, F-16-6).** Entity-source numbers run from 2^22, so `revid = number << 40 | n` is around 2^62, above JavaScript's safe-integer range; every browser, Node client and gadget will round `lastrevid`. Registry providers above 8191 hit the same wall. Recommendation: cap the ranged space at 53 bits — provider numbers below 2^13, entity-source numbers in a low reserved band — and add "every ID a client sees is below 2^53" to 01 §2.5 as a rule.

**R4. Unit, globe and calendar IRIs in stored JSON (F-06-4).** Canonical Wikibase JSON carries `unit`, `globe` and `calendarmodel` as concept IRIs under a base URI. For a local unit item the tenant's base is then written into the log on the first quantity statement, which contradicts 08 §6.1 ("projections emit every IRI from the current template; nothing is rewritten in the log") and leaves units outside `entity_ref` and canonical rewriting. Recommendation: the stored form carries these three fields as prefixed entity IDs (`"unit": "WDQ11573"`, `"unit": "Q7"`), expanded through the current template on output; the adapter already rewrites IDs on ingest, so the content hash is over the rewritten form. Say so in 06 and in `wikibase-compat.md`.

**R5. Bootstrapping the instance `config` partition (F-07-1).** Offset 0 holds the instance `key:` record, which must be attested by "an actor of the primary tenant" and checked for `ts-keys` held by `owner` whose first member `local:1` is created by `instance create` — none of which exists at offset 0. The next record, the tenant's key-chain copy, is a signed instance act, so the full prerogative machinery (tag `0x06`, authority field, `verify` level 2) is on the critical path of a single-tenant instance that 08 §1.1 promises "never notices any of this". Recommendation: offsets 0–2 are attested by `instance:{farm slug}`, signed by the key being registered (self-certifying), with `tenant:` and `primary:` citing offset 0 as authority; decide whether the key-chain copy is an ordinary `config` record attested by `local:1` on a single-tenant instance, upgraded to the signed form at the first rotation. (The milestone-1 code already does something here; whatever it does should be written down as the rule.)

**R6. Server-filled fields versus client signatures (F-13-6).** The thread `create` record carries the minted talk-page ID and the suffixed title in its content part (14 §1.3–1.4), and a sync job's `put` carries an allocated `n` (13 §5.4), but a client signature is over the content part as submitted (01 §2.4). Recommendation: a rule in 01 §2.4 that server-filled values live in the attestation map (already server-controlled) or a declared unsigned part, never in a signed part.

**R7. Three small permanent things (F-01-11, F-01-12, F-16-12).** The genesis record that names a partition's hash function, `k`, policies and name is cited in 01 §1.2 and §3.3 and specified nowhere; `log.segment_manifest.replaces` and `sealed_at` in its key contradict 01 §6 ("no manifest is re-signed and none lists a manifest it replaces"); whether a `hashed` partition has checkpoints and a partition root is said three ways (01 §1.2, §4.1, §4.2, 05 §3.8). And the rule for log IDs should be stated positively: every record in `local`, `pages`, the tenant `log` and `actors` partitions takes a `logid` at append whether or not the current catalogue projects it, because `activity_logid` is unique and a type that later gains an event cannot be retrofitted.

## 3. The multi-source core

These are the findings that bear directly on the next phase. Most are decisions rather than designs; several reviewers converged on the same recommendation from different chapters.

### 3.1 Canonical-ID rewriting should happen at read time, not in the stored view

(F-01-1, F-04-1, F-02-7; three groups, same conclusion.) 04 §4.4 has the resolved view rewrite subjects and entity values to the canonical ID, and 03 §4.2 materializes the rewritten JSON whenever it differs from the source. The consequence at scale: the set of entities whose resolved form differs from their source is not "entities in a cluster" but "entities in a cluster plus every entity that references a non-canonical member". With OpenAlex as a second provider, most works reference authors, institutions and sources that are clustered with Wikidata items, so most of the largest table becomes `materialized`, which is exactly the doubling 03 §4.2's `source` kind exists to avoid. Worse, tier-1 links are per tenant: a Librarybase `convert` or `same-as` on `WDQ5` (human) makes the local member canonical for Librarybase only, so every item with `P31 = WDQ5` needs a Librarybase overlay row in `entity`, `entity_ref` and its materialized JSON — one edit, ten million rows per tenant, plus one RDF delta per referrer. The 250 ms budget bounds the editor's latency, not the work, and the overlay stops being an overlay.

Recommendation: adopt Wikibase's own precedent for redirects. Stored and served entity *values* keep the ID the source wrote; the cluster map (small by construction, bounded by the number of links) lives in L0/L1 per tenant and is applied when a body is served, when RDF is emitted and when a search document is built; `owl:sameAs` for non-canonical members (already in 04 §4.4) lets consumers follow. Then a cluster change touches the cluster's members and nothing else, `materialized` is reserved for entities with two or more contributing graphs or local deltas, the overlay holds only entities a tenant asserted something *about*, and `entity_ref` becomes what "Links here" needs rather than what correctness needs (the Wikidata profile can then serve "Links here" from the query service and drop its largest table). 04 §4.9's statement-GUID rule already has this shape. This changes `view.entity`, `view.rdf_delta`, cache keys and the delta contract, so it must be decided before the resolution projection is extended for clusters. Related: F-16-7 — resolving a non-canonical member as a Wikibase *redirect* makes Pywikibot raise `IsRedirectPageError` on every clustered Wikidata item; a read-time rewrite lets `wbgetentities&ids=WDQ42` answer under the requested ID with a `canonical` field, and reserve the redirect shape for true redirects.

### 3.2 Per-graph current state is stored nowhere

(F-01-2, F-16-3.) Resolution is a function of each contributing graph's current state. For a mirror graph that is the latest `put`; for the local graph on a foreign entity, or any local entity edited with `add`/`remove`, no record holds the state — 03 §4.2 stores only the fused result, which cannot be un-fused when the mirror side changes. So every re-resolution of an entity with local edits, every `local` delta for the update stream (02 §6.2 reads "the graph's previous state"), and every `oldid=`, `action=compare`, `GET /entity/{id}/compare` and `?as_of=` must replay the key's local history from the log inside the request or the write transaction. Recommendation: add `view.graph_state (tenant, entity_id, graph, state, "offset", version)` written by the entity projection for every graph whose records are deltas, name it as the "old state" the delta projection reads, and decide separately whether per-revision local state is stored (`view.entity_revision`, MediaWiki's model, cheap because local edits are the small part of a mirror-dominated instance) or old revisions are served by replay from a periodic `snapshot` record with a cap.

### 3.3 Shared rows need instance-scope policy, and the tables need tenant columns where the overlay rule applies

(F-19-14, F-06-3, F-04-4, F-01-4, F-02-1, F-07-5.) 03 §7 keys overlays on records in the tenant's partitions. But a tenant `reconcile` record (provider order, link properties, normalizer overrides), a `providers` list, a `sitelink-policy`, a `role` binding and `entities.term_languages` are tenant configuration (23 §2.2, §3.2) and each changes the resolved result of every multi-source entity without a record in any tenant partition. There are only two implementations — materialize the whole view per such tenant, or re-resolve on read — and the chapters choose neither. At the same time the shared cluster tables (`cluster`, `cluster_member` with `entity_id UNIQUE`, `link`, `different_from`) and `view.entity_source` (PK `(entity_id, graph)`, holding per-tenant `local` rows) have no tenant column, so a tenant's tier-1 link on `WDQ42` cannot be stored beside the shared cluster, and two tenants with local assertions on one foreign entity collide. The quad store has the mirror-image problem (F-02-1): 02 §7.3 stores "every tenant's resolved graph" as a named graph and makes it the default graph of the tenant's dataset, so forty tenants mirroring Wikidata hold forty copies of Wikidata's resolved triples, not one. And 06 §2.4 computes shared-row constraint violations "once for the instance" with roles that only exist at tenant scope.

Recommendation, in one decision: shared rows are computed under **instance** policy — provider order among registry providers, tier-2 link properties, normalizers, the constraint roles and the languages that populate shared `view.term` are instance configuration (as inference properties already are, A17/B6); a tenant may *narrow* (hide a provider, restrict languages in its UI) and may add link properties only for provider tenants whose graphs are read per tenant anyway, but may not reorder shared providers or rebind shared roles. State in 03 §7 which config kinds may change the shared result and that a write to any other at tenant scope is refused. Give `cluster`, `cluster_member`, `link`, `different_from` and `entity_source` the `tenant` column with the same `''`-then-tenant lookup as `entity`. Write the overlay-exclusion rule once: a read over shared rows by any key other than the entity (identifier by value, sitelink by URL, `entity_ref` by target, term by prefix) excludes shared rows whose source entity the viewer's tenant has overlaid, and say which indexes make that cheap; for search, a tenant overlay document replaces the shared one through a `tenant_overlaid` term maintained by the overlay projection, not a post-query dedup. For the quad store, one shared resolved graph computed under instance policy plus small per-tenant overlay graphs, with 08 §9.1's "runs unchanged" promise kept for tenants with no overlay; defer the rest to the ADR that introduces the second tenant. The expensive-but-honest alternative, "resolution profiles" (one shared view per distinct policy tuple, tenants bound to a profile), should be named as the escape hatch.

### 3.4 Non-Wikibase adapters cannot write "local properties by role" into an instance partition

(F-04-3, F-06-2.) 04 §4.6 maps OpenAlex fields onto a Wikidata property where one fits, "otherwise onto a local property", bound by role, which "the adapter can create on first import"; GDELT and OSM do the same. But `mirror/{provider}` is an instance partition, `role` is tenant config, a local `P12` means a different entity on each tenant, and an instance job cannot mint from a tenant's `property_id` sequence. The OpenAlex, GDELT and OSM adapters as specified work only on a single-tenant instance, and OpenAlex is in the next phase. Recommendation: reintroduce a property type for providers that need one (`OAP…`), minted by the adapter under deterministic IDs with the mapping versioned with the adapter (04 §4.6 already says the mapping is versioned); it is a registry change plus one `[[provider.type]]` row, and it keeps the mirror shared and the adapter instance-scoped. If a registry-provider property is the only allowed target, say so and accept the data loss. Either way, an instance job must not read a tenant's `role` records; adapters need instance-scope role bindings (the same instance `role` record 06 §2.4 needs) or none. Also F-04-11: deterministic statement IDs for sources without them should hash the main snak and adapter-declared *identifying* qualifiers (position, institution, award), never score-like ones, or OpenAlex authorships collide.

### 3.5 `view.term` under a full mirror

(F-04-6, F-01-10; already flagged in `adoption-results-and-wikidata-projection.md` §2.) 05 §5.8 leaves "what a full mirror writes to `view.term`" as "Not yet", and the setting that bounds it, `entities.term_languages`, is a *tenant* site setting while the rows it bounds are shared. Wikidata's term store is on the order of 10^9 rows; this is the `wb_terms` problem and it decides the shape of the label-lookup API and the search projection. Recommendation: `view.term` holds rows for local entities, for entities in any tenant overlay, and for mirrored entities only in the *instance's* `term_languages`; every other label lookup reads the record body at `entity_source.offset` through the L1 label cache 05 §5.6 already defines; `wbsearchentities` at scale is the search index, as 00 §2.8 already says; the `term_prefix` index is never created on an instance holding a full mirror (or is partial over local entities, which 21 §2.2's "local by default" wants anyway). State the budget: rows ≈ mirrored entities × |instance languages| × term kinds. Separately, key `entity_ref` on integers (`page_id` is already a unique bigint per entity) with a role bitmask and drop `statement_id` from it; at 10^9 rows the text-keyed index as specified is a few hundred GB and is rewritten on every re-resolution.

### 3.6 A bulk path into a live partition

(F-04-2, F-01-6, F-01-8; the throughput doc's items 2–4 are the same work.) The only bulk mode is bootstrap into an *empty* partition with no `view` tables (05 §3.8); the paths the next phase will actually walk — `linked` → `all`, weekly re-syncs of 5–15M changed entities, adding OpenAlex to a live instance — go through the per-partition row lock one transaction at a time, with projections 1–5 inside it. Recommendation: specify a third mode, bulk append into a live partition: a job takes the partition lock once per block (`ops.bootstrap_block` already has the shape), `COPY`s the block, folds the block's leaves from the in-memory frontier, and the projection worker applies steps 2–4 per block with `ops.projection_state` advancing per block; the `put` skip (version cursor) is evaluated in bulk against `entity_source` before the block is written, so a re-sync's unchanged entities never reach the lock. Specify per-partition catch-up: a new provider's partition is loaded in bootstrap mode and then projected against the existing `view`, with its tier-2 and fusion fan-out queued. And decide the interactive ceiling (F-01-8): gapless offsets force the lock to be held until commit, and commit comes after every synchronous projection plus up to 250 ms of fan-out, so one tenant's interactive edits serialize behind each other's projections at single digits per second under load. Option (b) in F-01-8 — allocate and append under the lock, commit, then apply projections 1–5 in a second transaction keyed by the record, with read-your-writes served by the version the response returns — keeps every invariant in 01 and moves the budget out of the lock; whatever is chosen, say which projections may run after the append commits and which must not (the ACL projection and `filter` are read by the write path).

### 3.7 What a Wikibase write *means* on a multi-source entity, and what `baserevid` checks

(F-16-1, F-16-2, F-04-10.) This is the acceptance test of the next phase more than any page is, and it is not written down. WikidataIntegrator and Pywikibot send the whole entity — the full `claims` list as fetched, GUIDs included; if Triplespace merges that as `add` operations, every bot write copies every mirrored statement into the local graph, the stale-copy problem 05 and 04 §4.9 reject. A changed value on a mirror-owned GUID has no representable outcome (`override` changes rank, a term, or suppresses; not a value). `clear=true`, `wbremoveclaims` on a mirror GUID, `wbsetaliases` removing a mirrored alias and `wbsetsitelink` replacing a mirrored link each need one sentence. On conflicts, 01 §7 checks `baserevid` "across the source partitions", so on a live mirror every local write to an actively edited item is a spurious `editconflict` — on popular items the conflict rate equals Wikidata's edit rate and clients, which expect Wikibase's patching, fail rather than retry; and 01 §2.5's `lastrevid` "chosen by append time" is neither monotonic nor from one space, while 03 §4.2's `local_revid` column says `lastrevid` is the local revision. Recommendation: a section "Wikibase writes against the resolved view" in 18 §2 fixing (1) every `wb*` write is diffed against the tenant's resolved view and unchanged content is dropped; (2) a table from module and submitted shape to operation, by whether the touched GUID/term/sitelink is local-owned or mirror-owned, with the value-change-on-mirror-GUID case decided once (refusal with `ts-foreign-statement`, or suppress-and-add returning the new GUID); (3) `clear` clears local assertions only; (4) the response is the re-resolved entity. For conflicts: `lastrevid` is the latest local revision when any local record exists, else the ranged ID of the newest mirror record; the base check is per source graph (a mirror advancing is never a conflict with a local assertion); adopt Wikibase's patch rule for the Action API (`wikibase-conflict-patched` when the change set touches nothing a later local record touched); define inbound `maxlag` as the larger of replica lag and local projection lag so bots throttle during bulk loads.

### 3.8 Scope the constraint projection

(F-01-7, F-06-1.) 06 §2.2 checks mirrored data "as local data is", re-checks `type` constraints on every referrer when an item's `P31` changes, re-scans a whole property when a constraint statement changes, and runs the projection as a default step over 110M entities at bootstrap. Wikidata's own checker is a WDQS-backed, days-long, sampled job that does not do this. Recommendation: `constraints.scope = local | overlay | all` with `local` the default (local-graph statements and entities a tenant has touched); `all` is an opt-in batch job with progress, never a projection step; constraint-statement changes on mirrored properties enqueue a batch; `type` is either checked at display time with the L0 class cache or its staleness under `P279` changes is documented. No table changes.

### 3.9 The update stream: off the write path, and a safe cursor

(F-01-5, F-02-2, F-02-3, F-02-5, F-16-4.) Three distinct problems. First, cost: the delta projection renders old and new state to RDF twice and diffs them inside every ingest transaction, whether or not a consumer exists; it is the largest per-record cost in the projection chain for a feature the next phase does not use. Second, correctness: `rdf_delta.seq` is an identity allocated at insert time, so under concurrent commits (interactive edits, sync batches, the fan-out worker) a consumer reading `seq > cursor` skips a late-committing row forever; the "one transaction is one event" rule has the same hole. The activity stream's cursor (16 §4.4, 18 §1.3) is ordered by *source time*, which is worse — imported revisions, adopted history and upstream events all carry old timestamps — and 17 §1.2's verified federation follows that stream. Third, two sentences on rebuild contradict: 03 §4.11 says `rdf_delta` is replayed with every other `view` table, 02 §6.2 says the delta "is stored, not recomputed"; replaying a `latest` mirror through the delta projection emits all of Wikidata into a 30-day window. Recommendation: the delta projection is a configured consumer (`updates.enabled`, default off) that runs in the projection worker behind the append, reading old state from the `graph_state` table of §3.2; `seq` is assigned by a single sequencer (the worker) so insert order is commit order, or rows are served only below the oldest in-flight transaction; the activity stream's resume token is a vector of per-partition high-water marks (offsets are commit-ordered under the append lock) and (time, partition, offset) stays for paged display; a rebuild truncates `rdf_delta` and bumps the epoch; and the epoch also carries a *rendering version* that a renderer or normalizer change increments, because `DELETE DATA` only matches byte-identical triples and nothing today versions the lexical forms. Per-tenant epochs (F-02-8) let a tenant that opts in to a provider tell only its own consumers to reload.

### 3.10 Smaller core items

Retention and tombstones across the instance/tenant boundary (F-04-5): a mirror tombstone is one record in a shared partition but retention is per tenant per entity, so "materialized in the same log record" cannot hold, `cascade` is an unlabelled instance act, and a shallow fetch miss for a typo'd ID tombstones a never-present entity and retracts the editor's statement. Make the entity projection apply the tenant's policy per overlay as a tenant-partition record with an authority record, define compaction exemption as "any tenant's `retain` row", never tombstone on a fetch miss, and consider `orphan` as the default. Keyed-type surrogates (F-04-7): minted through the single instance `log` partition (a key-mapped bootstrap of millions of keys becomes millions of serialized appends), with no lock against two writers meeting a new key, and erased instance-wide by one tenant's privacy erasure; allocate under an advisory lock in the appending transaction, let bulk jobs reserve blocks, and scope erasure to "no live record in any partition is keyed to the surrogate". `snapshot` mode (F-04-8) cannot tell unchanged from absent because skipped `put`s append nothing; add `seen_job` on `entity_source` written by the ingester. Hosting a provider as a tenant (F-04-9, the owner's own first case): nothing says how an instance knows whether `LB` is a remote wiki to sync or a tenant here; make it instance configuration and specify the freeze of a pre-existing mirror partition. Registry (F-04-12): the `XD` row says `provider-only` while 05 §8.1's table says `individual` (internetdomains has individual editors; fix the row); strike "may add providers" from the README in favour of entity sources; add slug and per-type IRI template to the never-changed list. Sitelink uniqueness across graphs (F-06-6) is a primary-key conflict inside a sync batch as written; local wins per URL and the projection never fails. Normalizer changes (F-06-5) need a per-property rebuild job and a version on `value_key`. Derived graphs (F-13-10) are a tenant source that 03 §7's overlay triggers do not list.

## 4. Projection discipline: rules chapter 03 should state once

Several chapters do things to projections that 03 §6.1 does not allow for, and the fixes are rules rather than designs.

**No side effects during replay.** `view.blob` queues byte destruction "at once" when a count reaches zero (12 §5.3), so a replay from offset 0 walks through historical zero-counts and deletes bytes the live head still references (F-10-4, permanent loss). Proposal adoption inside the mirror projection appends a `remove` record as an instance act (14 §5.4), so a rebuild re-appends every retirement (F-13-4). Rule: a projection may enqueue work in `ops`, never append a record or destroy bytes; destruction and retirement are jobs that re-check the live head under a lock, with an idempotency key, and the queue is not a projection target.

**A view-derived projection class.** A projection added after the mirror exists (constraints, reports, scopes, `entity_usage`, `site_stats`, schema reports) starts at `applied_offset = 0` and replays hundreds of millions of records to produce a few rows about local entities (F-19-2); 21 §2.3 already quietly exempts reports. Rule: a projection declares whether it replays from the log or rebuilds from named `view` tables; the second class is populated by a scan of those tables and "rebuild projection P for tenant T from `view`" is a named operation (21 §2.3 asks for it).

**Rebuild by swap, with an epoch.** "Truncate and replay" takes the site down for the duration of an `entity` rebuild at Wikidata scale, and after it `resolved_version` restarts so a pre-rebuild L1 key can match a different body (F-01-6). Rule: large projections rebuild into `view_next` and swap; every rebuild bumps an instance-wide cache epoch that is part of every L1 key.

**Replay order across partitions and concurrency with the inline path.** Whether `create` at a title is legal depends on the interleaving of the `pages` record and the delete ACL in the tenant `log` (F-10-5); task credit and proposal state depend on the interleaving of mirror and local partitions (F-13-7); autopatrol "as of the record's time" compares microsecond timestamps across partitions and breaks after a vanish erases memberships (F-07-7). Rule: a tenant's partitions replay in commit order (record the transaction sequence in `ops` or merge by `(appended_at, partition, offset)` with the tie-break stated), or the affected projections are declared non-rebuildable and derived from `view.activity`; and put decisions the write path already knows (autopatrolled, operator) into the attestation part so rebuilds are trivially stable. Concurrency (F-01-9): resolution of a key takes `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on its `view.entity` row in both the inline path and the worker; L1 is written after commit (or `resolved_version` is drawn from an instance-wide sequence so a rolled-back version is never reused); `ops.projection_work` is the unit of truth for lag and `applied_offset` records the contiguous prefix. Multi-process append (F-01-13): the tree's right edge is read under the append lock, and checkpoints are signed by one elected process.

**Two writers, one table.** `page_category`, `page_link` and `page_prop` are written by the page projection in step 2 with expansion off and by the refresh job with expansion on, and "a property the latest render did not set is deleted" (F-10-3). Rule: one writer per row class — source-derived rows belong to the projection, expansion-derived rows to the refresh job (a `from_render` column or separate tables unioned by readers); the refresh job never deletes a row it did not write.

## 5. Costs outside the core that are cheap to decide now

These are design debts that do not block the next phase but will be expensive if the mechanisms they touch ship first. One paragraph each; the detailed reports have the full argument and suggestion.

**Visibility epoch and denormalized visibility sets (F-07-3).** Public cache entries are keyed `-` with no epoch, so a page cached as public before its namespace was restricted stays served until TTL (exactly 09 §5.8 case 7); read the other way, every set-membership change cold-starts the tenant's cache. `read_groups` on every activity row, search document and inbox row is an O(namespace) rewrite per ACL change and goes silently stale when a restriction with an expiry lapses. Decide: public entries carry `-{epoch}` for tenant keys; only `namespace` and `tenant` ACLs bump the epoch, `set` changes bump the generation of the touched members; stored `read_groups` becomes a reference to the enclosures evaluated at serve time from L0. Build the `visibility(target)` hook and `{vis}` on day one regardless.

**Registry copies in `config` with no upgrade path (F-07-6, F-19-15).** Groups, presets, grants, tag prefixes and preferences are copied from the registry into `config` records at create; release N+1 adding `ts-foo` reaches no existing tenant. 23 §4.1's "starts from the registry" is undefined between copy-on-create and overlay-at-read, and the two produce different logs. Decide copy-on-create plus a `registry sync` command that appends records for newer registry entries under the instance attestation; group records store deltas against a named registry version; every config record carries a per-kind `schema` integer and an unknown kind or schema is projected opaquely so an older binary can replay a newer log.

**Tenant settings that are capacity knobs (F-19-16).** By the 23 §3.1 rule, `projections.sync_budget`, `projections.sync_time`, `files.thumb_concurrency`, `repo.concurrency`, `query.timeout` and a dozen others are tenant site settings; a tenant on a community farm can hold the append lock for everyone. Add a "bound" column to 23 §3.2 — tenant, tenant ≤ instance ceiling, instance — and move pool sizes to deployment configuration. `query.isolation` should be instance-only.

**The entity page as N API calls (F-19-7, F-19-8, F-19-18).** A logged-in reader opening a large item is 10–40 API round trips (entity, provenance, labels in batches of 50, userinfo, tokens, inbox, scopes, proposals), each through session lookup, the `read` rate counter and visibility; a reading room behind one NAT address is rate-limited after a few hundred page views; the provenance response carries a backlink count that is `COUNT(*)` over `entity_ref` for `WDQ5`; and the `Cache-Tag` union over every label source exceeds every CDN's tag limit. Decide a per-page budget (say four calls to first byte) met by a bulk-labels route cacheable by language and a `GET /entity/{id}/page` bundle; exempt the in-process transport from counting; maintain `ref_count` and revision counts as columns updated in batches and render "10,000+" above a threshold; tag composed pages with their subjects and render-manifest pages only.

**History of a mirrored entity (F-19-19).** 19 §3.3–3.5, 16 §3.2 and 03 §4.6 describe its rows three ways (activity rows; mirror records by key across member partitions; upstream log events, which 16 puts in `view.activity` and 03 says never grow it). Name one backing, `view.entity_history`, written by the resolution projection, and settle whether upstream log events are activity rows. Related: the default mirrored Wikidata log (`create/create` for every entity ever created, F-16-5) is a second 10^8-record Merkle partition the next phase does not need; default the mirrored set to `delete/*` and `protect/*`, narrow the backfill to held entities, and derive `create/create` from the first upstream revision.

**Entity-usage classification on every entity change (F-10-2).** 11 §7.9 classifies every `resolved_version` change from its structured diff before checking whether any page uses the entity; at sync rates that is a diff per change for a usually-empty table. Reverse the order (indexed existence check first), run it off the event stream, and skip it in bootstrap mode or while no tenant has expansion on. The same shape recurs for scope maintenance (F-13-5: the trigger set cannot find `column` or `pages_of` scopes, truncation is not incremental, and every mirror `put` consults it), schema validation (F-13-8: a ShEx run with a two-hop walk per `put` of every human on a Wikidata mirror, inside the fan-out budget), edit filters on bulk ingest (F-07-8: a full context per record and one hit record per match into the tenant `log`), ACL lookups per resolution (F-07-9: one indexed miss per entity per resolution, with a `property` read ACL on `P31` fanning out to 10^8 generation bumps), and `commonsMedia` validation (F-10-9: an upstream HTTP call inside a local edit, including bulk jobs). The common rule, worth writing once in 03 §6.2: the inline path and the fan-out budget carry only what read-your-writes and base checks need; everything that is a function of *other* tenants' or *other* features' state is a queued consumer with its own worker, rate and lag, skipped in bootstrap and bulk modes, with a per-tenant cap refused at configuration time where the product of two sizes can explode.

**Fork history seeding (F-13-3).** Defaults seed up to 20,000 text-bearing revisions per article plus every dependency's and archive's history into the tenant `log` partition — terabytes at MDWiki scale — and `view.upstream_revision`'s key `(provider, upstream_revid)` has no tenant column, so two tenants forking one article collide. Default to metadata-only history with content for the last N, a per-job budget, and a tenant-aware key.

**Instance-scope page artefacts keyed by tenant configuration (F-13-2, F-13-11, F-13-12).** The title index, bundles and `fp:` keys are instance scope keyed by repository *name*, but `page-repo` is tenant config and the HTML rewrite depends on tenant state; the first tenant to fetch decides what every tenant serves and, in mirror mode, what is logged. Make page repositories instance-level (one per provider; tenants select), the stored rewrite tenant-independent, and the tenant pass a cheap step at serve time.

**The crate map as a build plan (F-19-1, F-19-2, F-19-3, F-19-4, F-19-5, F-19-11).** The table already violates rule 1 (`scatter-extract` in the substrate depends on `scatter-wikibase-model`; `scatter-log` and `scatter-pages` carry Triplespace-only payloads and config kinds; `scatter-wikibase-model` transitively links a CSS parser), so `cargo xtask deps` fails against it or the rule has been amended by accretion; the three store traits share a transaction and no transaction type (give `LogStore` an associated unit-of-work type that `Backend::apply_in` and `IngestStore` take; state RPITIT with `+ Send` and `spawn_blocking` for the file backend); rule 10 forbids `triplespace-ui` from the only crate that embeds `namespaces.toml` and `special-pages.toml` (split a pure `scatter-titles`); and the wasm rule names nine crates and ignores their closure (state the closure rule and a byte budget, split the editor bundle in two). Since milestone 1 already compiles a subset, 22 §3.1 should say that the projection order is a partial order over registered projections and define the subset by milestone.

**Minor contradictions worth a line each** (full text in the reports): subsidiary approval stated three ways and `basic` versus `read` grant for MCP (F-07-11); `autoconfirmed` needs an edit count `view.actor` lacks (F-07-12); `read` rate counting on every API read is a Valkey round trip in front of L0 hits (F-07-10); a move must create a `wikitext` redirect in namespaces that forbid `wikitext` (F-10-6); "local overlay on an inherited file" is the entry kind 13 §1.7 refuses and `file-repo` has no provider number to range from (F-10-7); captions add a `terms` operation the change-set format does not need and key the subject two ways (F-10-8); on-demand MediaInfo mirroring lets an anonymous page view write a mirror partition (F-10-11); no stale serving after a dependency epoch bump means a template edit on a fork is a synchronous Lua render per page (F-10-12); `private.watch` cannot serve the `watch` addressing rule without a target index (F-16-9); `private.upstream_grant` stores hashes of tokens the instance must present (F-16-10); verified federation bootstraps by one record fetch per entity (F-16-11); `siprop=triplespace` mixes configuration with live counters (F-16-8); served special pages reported as existing before they are implemented (F-19-10); entity reports evaluated on the resolved view fan every mirror term change through the report projection (F-19-12); the skolem IRI's hash inputs are unnamed and one of them is rewritten by resolution (F-02-6); ## 6. On over-specification

The brief asked where detail is a trap rather than merely detail. The reviewers' answer, which I share: the DDL and the key catalogues are not traps — 03 §12 already says the catalogue is the index, not the authority, and the column lists will drift harmlessly. The traps are where a *format* is pinned by an example rather than a rule (the actor key spelling, the quantity unit IRI, the skolem hash), where a *mechanism* is stated as a consequence of a sentence in one ADR and silently contradicts a sentence in another (page IDs, `lastrevid`, the delta rebuild), and where a catalogue has become the *implementation plan* (22 §2.1's `triplespace-projections` row, 21 §8's `served` set). None of these argues for less text. They argue for the chapters to carry three things the ADRs did not need: an explicit list of what is permanent, an explicit scope column (instance / tenant / tenant-bounded) on everything configurable, and an explicit class on every projection (inline / queued / job; log-replayed / view-derived; side-effect-free).

## 8. Sequencing the next phase

The reviewers' sequencing notes agree with each other and with `milestone-1-critical-path.md`'s "deliberately off the path" list. Build next, in this order: the format decisions R1–R7 (small, and they gate everything); bulk append and per-partition catch-up (R13, the throughput doc's items 2–4); the full Wikidata load with terms restricted (R12) and no constraint, delta, filter, scope or validation projections running on the mirror partition (R16, R18, R19); clusters with read-time rewriting (R8, R9, R10); the Wikibase write semantics and conflict rules with the three client libraries as the test (R14, R15); OpenAlex after R11; then the site UI's entity page with its call budget.
